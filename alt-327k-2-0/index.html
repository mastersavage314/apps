How Gaia is built

Gaia's air follows its locked numbers: 123.262 kPa at the sea, 55.000 kPa at 8,400 m, 21% oxygen, 130°F at the coast, and 5.2°C cooler with every kilometer you climb. Above Mount Ohwenroken no temperature is locked yet, so the sandbox keeps cooling the air until it hits −70°F, the temperature of Earth's stratosphere, and holds it there.

SpO2 is a simulation, not a medical reading. Gaian lungs are fitted to the Gaians' own lines: a Gaian breathes like someone from Earth does 7,560 m lower, all the way up to the living ceiling at 13,060 m, and the Gaian death zone starts at 16,000 m. The Earth visitor is an acclimatized adult from Earth breathing Gaia's outside air with no gear. Performance time reads the FAA's table at the matching Earth altitude.

The visitor's gear follows the locked survival kit. Between average ground and Summitbrook, the switch from a tube to a full mask sits where the air is as thin as Earth's at 18,000 ft, the height where the FAA stops allowing nasal tubes.

Every elevation here is its Earth elevation plus 7,560 m. Dry land and lake surfaces below sea level are 10 times deeper, and the oceans are 1.25 times deeper. That goes for every real place the arrows step through. Coalinga, Ospero, and Cyrillo are shown at the surface of the lake they sit on.

Air numbers come from the 1976 US Standard Atmosphere, which is a yearly average. Real weather pushes them around. Below sea level, they assume a dry shaft open to the sky, and the ocean tile shows the real water pressure.

SpO2 is a simulation for an acclimatized adult at rest breathing outside air. It is not a medical reading. Performance time follows the FAA's Advisory Circular 61-107B and is smoothed between the altitudes it lists.

The real places use their commonly published elevations: the official figure for a city or town, the summit for a mountain, the water's surface for a lake, and the sea floor for a trench or a wreck. Lake levels and ice caps shift a little from year to year. Cities and mountains outside Colorandoa and Apōpō are as they are on Earth.

Elevations outside Colorandoa follow Wikipedia's list of highest settlements, which covers year-round settlements at 3,500 m and up and says itself that it's incomplete. Colorandoa numbers are the locked ones. Coalinga, Ospero, and Cyrillo are shown at the surface of the lake they sit on, since their own elevations aren't locked yet.
